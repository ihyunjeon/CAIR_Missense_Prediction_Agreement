CAIR Summer 2026 Design Review 26 Aug 2026

Structure or Frequency

A review of the AlphaMissense × ESM-1v disagreement study, plus the compute plan to actually run it. The core idea is publishable. As currently specified, the central question is not identifiable from the data — and one of the two models was never going to run locally in the first place.

00

Read this before anything else

Premise correction

AlphaMissense cannot be run locally, or on Hoffman2, or anywhere. DeepMind released the data-pipeline and model code as an implementation reference, but explicitly did not release the trained weights. What they released instead is the precomputed score for every possible human missense variant — all ~71M of them.

So the pipeline is one model you run (ESM-1v) and one model you look up (AlphaMissense). This is good news: it halves the GPU work. It also relocates the hard part of this project from compute to data engineering, which is where the real schedule risk now lives.

Local blocker — fix on day one

The MacBook has 195 MB free of 460 GB. Nothing in this project's data plan fits. ~/Downloads (16 GB) and ~/Library/Caches (11 GB) are the obvious reclaims. Regardless, bulk data belongs on $SCRATCH, not the laptop — the disk state just forces the correct architecture earlier than it otherwise would have.

Asset you already have

Reference_Repos/condenseq-esm-handoff contains a working, hard-won Hoffman2 GPU scaffold: env.sh, check_access.sh, array-job templates with lockfile skip-guards, and a documented playbook of every dispatch failure mode on this cluster. Lift it wholesale. It encodes roughly a week of debugging you do not need to repeat. tools/mmseqs is also already installed, which covers the paralog-clustering step for the train/test splits.

01

Where the design breaks

Fatal

The AM vs ESM-1v contrast confounds three variables at once

The plan frames this as a two-way choice: structure (A) versus frequency prior (B). But these two models differ along three axes simultaneously, and every disagreement you observe is jointly caused by all three.

PredictorStructureExplicit MSAFrequency supervision
AlphaMissenseyesyes — AF2 Evoformeryes
ESM-1vnono — single sequenceno

With one contrast and three differences, no downstream feature-importance analysis can separate them. This is an identification problem in the experimental design, not a statistical problem you can model your way out of.

The fix is cheap, and it is the single highest-value change to the plan. Add predictors that break the confound — all have precomputed scores you can download, so the marginal cost is disk, not GPU:

  • EVE or GEMME — MSA-based, no structure, no frequency supervision
  • PrimateAI-3D — structure + frequency supervision, different architecture

Now AM − EVE isolates roughly (structure + frequency supervision) with MSA held constant, and EVE − ESM-1v isolates the alignment axis. The headline contrast stops being "one model minus another model" and becomes a real factorial design.

Fatal

Your labels are themselves frequency-derived

ACMG classification criteria BA1 and BS1 assign "benign" on the basis of gnomAD allele frequency exceeding a threshold. A large share of ClinVar's benign calls are benign because they are common.

The consequence is severe: gnomAD frequency will predict the label, predict AlphaMissense, and predict AM–ESM disagreement — all through the label, not through AlphaMissense's fine-tuning. Step 3 as specified will report "the confound group dominates" regardless of which explanation is true.

Two things follow. First, ClinVar density does not belong in the confound group at all — AlphaMissense did not train on ClinVar, so it is a confound of your labels, not of the model. Keep it, but label it as an ascertainment covariate. Second, you need a label source with no frequency signal in it:

The fix

Use ProteinGym / MaveDB deep-mutational-scanning data as a second primary evaluation set. DMS fitness scores are experimental measurements — uncontaminated by allele frequency and by which genes clinicians happened to sequence. When AM and ESM disagree, ask which one is right against the experiment, stratified by structure. That question is identifiable; the ClinVar-only version is not.

Major

Permutation importance cannot answer "how much is attributable"

Adding noise to a feature and measuring the performance drop measures unique non-redundant predictive contribution within one fitted model. It is not causal attribution, and under correlated features it is unstable — repeated runs will give different answers.

Your two groups are correlated by construction. Buried residues are conserved. Disordered regions are unconstrained. Worse: conservation belongs in both groups, not the confound group. AlphaMissense sees conservation through its MSA path, and ESM-1v encodes it implicitly — masked marginals are a learned evolutionary prior over UniRef. Calling conservation importance "evidence for Explanation B" is a misattribution.

Replace with: grouped conditional permutation (permute the structural block jointly, within strata of the confound block); LOCO with refitting rather than noise injection on a fixed model; and report the variance decomposition as a Venn — structure-only, confound-only, and the shared overlap.

If the overlap is 60%, that is your result. "The majority of AM–ESM disagreement cannot be attributed to structure or frequency separately, because they are collinear at residue level" is a real, publishable, honest finding. Do not let the analysis manufacture a split that is not in the data.

Major

Step 2's comparison is circular

Your structure-only model is trained on ClinVar. AlphaMissense and ESM-1v are not. Comparing their AUROCs on the same test set is not apples-to-apples, and your model will look artificially competitive. It can also reach AUROC ~0.75 largely by memorising "this gene's known hotspot domain" — Fawzy & Marsh's circularity concern, operating at residue level.

Mitigations: gene-held-out splits (you have this); report macro-averaged per-gene AUROC, not pooled AUROC, since pooled is dominated by between-gene base-rate variation; and add a gene-identity-only baseline. If gene-ID-only gets 0.70 and your structural model gets 0.74, you have learned almost nothing about structure.

Six smaller problems worth fixing

25 genes is too few

With gene-held-out CV your effective n for variance purposes is ~25, and ClinVar's confident labels are extraordinarily gene-skewed — BRCA1/2, CFTR, TP53 and the mismatch-repair genes will supply most of it. Confidence intervals on AUROC differences will be uselessly wide. There is no compute reason for the limit, since AM scores are precomputed and AlphaFold DB ships the whole human proteome. Target 300–500 genes; keep 25 well-characterised genes as a sensitivity analysis.

The score scales are not comparable

AlphaMissense outputs a calibrated pathogenicity probability with published cutoffs; ESM-1v outputs an unbounded log-likelihood ratio whose scale depends on the protein's MSA entropy and family depth. Raw disagreement is partly a calibration artifact. Rank-normalise both within protein before comparing. Non-negotiable.

Define disagreement continuously, and keep the sign

Binary agree/disagree after arbitrary thresholding throws away most of the signal. Use the signed residual of within-protein ESM rank regressed on within-protein AM rank. Sign matters: "AM says pathogenic, ESM does not" is a different phenomenon from the reverse, and collapsing them will cancel real effects.

MSA depth is probably your largest single driver, and it is in neither group

ESM-1v degrades on shallow-family and rarely-observed proteins; AlphaMissense has an explicit alignment. Compute Neff per protein and put it in the model. If it dominates both groups, that is an important negative result about the A-vs-B framing itself.

ESM-1v's 1022-token limit will manufacture fake disagreement

Proteins longer than that must be cropped, and the score depends on how. Use a fixed-size window centred on the variant, and record a cropped flag on every row — cropped variants will generate spurious disagreement and you need to be able to stratify it out.

Gene-level splitting is necessary but not sufficient

Paralogs leak across the split. Cluster by sequence identity with mmseqs2 at ~30% (already installed in tools/) or at minimum by Pfam family, and split on clusters.

One concern in the original plan is misdirected

"Can we reliably compare feature attribution when ESM is hard to interpret?" — you never need to interpret ESM's internals. It is an outcome variable here, not something you are explaining. The real attribution problem is collinearity among your features, above.

02

The revised question

Reframed

When structure-aware and sequence-only predictors disagree about a missense variant, which one is right — and does the answer depend on the residue's structural context in a way that survives conditioning on evolutionary constraint and allele frequency?

That version is identifiable. The original — "how much of the disagreement can be attributed to structural features" — is not, for the reasons in 01.

0Week 1

Feasibility spike

Before anyone builds a dataset: download the AM table, pull five AlphaFold structures, run SASA/DSSP, score five proteins with ESM-1v, and produce one fully joined row end to end. Every downstream decision depends on this working. Also audit what is already precomputed — if ProteinGym ships ESM-1v scores, some GPU work disappears entirely.

Owner — Ihyun

1Data

Build the joined variant table

ClinVar ≥2-star P/LP vs B/LB, MANE Select transcripts only. Scores: AlphaMissense, ESM-1v, plus EVE/GEMME and PrimateAI-3D. Structure from AlphaFold DB: DSSP 3-state, RSA (Tien et al. max-ASA normalisation), pLDDT, weighted contact number (better-behaved than RSA), distance to nearest UniProt functional site, Pfam domain membership. Covariates: gnomAD AF and observed/unobserved status, LOEUF, regional missense constraint, Neff, ClinVar density, protein length, crop flag. Second label set: ProteinGym DMS. 300+ genes.

Owners — Labels owner (ClinVar, labels) · Ihyun (scores, structure, joins)

2Baseline

Structure-only classifier, honestly benchmarked

Gene-held-out grouped CV, macro-averaged per-gene AUROC, bootstrap-over-genes CIs, against a gene-ID-only null. The question is not "can structure predict pathogenicity" (weakly, yes) — it is "does structure add anything over the gene-level prior."

Owner — Baseline owner · ties to Fawzy & Marsh 2024

3Core

Which model is right, stratified by structure

On the DMS set, restrict to disagreement cases and ask which predictor better tracks experimental fitness, stratified by buried/exposed, ordered/disordered, high/low pLDDT. This is the experiment that actually adjudicates A vs B, and it is clean because DMS labels carry no frequency signal. This is also your minimum publishable result.

Owner — DMS owner · ties to Fawzy & Marsh 2025

4Model

Disagreement model with honest attribution

Predict the signed, rank-normalised disagreement residual. Grouped conditional permutation importance, LOCO refitting, and a reported variance decomposition with the unattributable overlap made explicit rather than silently split.

Owner — Modelling owner · ties to Jonsson 2026

5Stress

Confound stress test

Stratify everything by gnomAD-observed vs unobserved. Explanation B predicts the structural stratification collapses once frequency status is conditioned on; Explanation A predicts it survives. Repeat on the DMS set as the frequency-free replication.

Owner — Ihyun

Scope

As originally written this is roughly 2× what five students finish in a summer. The staged version above has a defensible result at the end of Phase 3, with Phases 4–5 as upside rather than as load-bearing.

The bug that will silently corrupt everything

Four ID systems have to line up: ClinVar (RefSeq transcript coords) ↔ AlphaMissense (UniProt + genome coords) ↔ AlphaFold (UniProt accession) ↔ ESM-1v (whatever sequence you feed it). MANE Select is the pivot, but transcript-version drift, isoform mismatch and 0/1-indexing errors all fail quietly.

One assertion catches nearly all of them. Run it on every joined row, count and log the failures, and never let it be a warning:

# the single most valuable test in this project
assert af_seq[pos-1] == clinvar_ref_aa == am_ref_aa, (uniprot, pos)

If more than a few percent of rows fail, your join is wrong — do not proceed to modelling on the survivors, because the survivors are a biased subset.

Use DuckDB for every join. It reads gzipped TSV directly, spills out-of-core, and writes parquet — a 71M-row join in pandas will thrash on any machine you have. Predicate-push your gene list down into the scan and the AM tables become tractable even on the laptop.

05

Running ESM-1v on Hoffman2

The optimisation that sets your whole budget

One masked forward pass at position i returns the full 20-amino-acid log-probability vector at i — which means every variant at that residue comes free. Cost therefore scales with unique (protein, position) pairs, not with variant count. Cache the full vector, never a single score.

QuantityEstimateNote
Unique (protein, position) pairs, 300–500 genes~30–60kdrives everything below
Masked forward passes (×5 ensemble)~150–300kbatched ~16 seqs × ~1k tokens
Total GPU time, full ensemble~5–15 hon one A100; measure on 5 proteins first
Wall time as a -t 1-20 array< 1 hembarrassingly parallel by protein

This is a small job. If your pilot extrapolates to hundreds of GPU-hours, something is wrong — most likely you are re-running masked passes per variant instead of per position.

Cluster specifics, from the condenseq playbook

Two rules that cost real days last time

Never pass -q on a GPU job. A queue list combined with a generic -l gpu is a conflicting request and the job sits in qw indefinitely with no error message. Request the GPU model through -l instead. (IDRE ticket #100868426.)

Do not pass highp unless you are in the group ACL. A non-member's job waits forever, silently. Ihyun's highp access comes from the Kappel allocation — CAIR teammates on their own accounts almost certainly do not have it. Default the whole team to the public queue and let anyone who has highp opt in explicitly. Verify with bash hoffman2/check_access.sh before trusting it.

# the request that works
qsub -t 1-20 -l gpu,A100,cuda=1 hoffman2/esm1v_score.qsub

# free GPUs right now — qstat -g c shows free CPU slots, which is not the
# same thing. The cuda RSMAP consumable is what qw actually waits on.
ssh hoffman2 "qhost -F cuda | awk '/^g/{h=\$1} /hc:cuda=[1-9]/{print h,\$0}'"

# will this job EVER run? ('no suitable queues' = disabled queue or ACL)
qalter -w v <jobid>
Job script skeleton — adapted from the condenseq template 
#!/bin/bash
#$ -S /bin/bash
#$ -cwd -j y
#$ -o logs/esm1v.$JOB_ID.$TASK_ID.out
#$ -l h_rt=8:00:00
#$ -l h_data=10G
#$ -pe shared 4
#$ -N esm1v
# submit: qsub -t 1-20 -l gpu,A100,cuda=1 hoffman2/esm1v_score.qsub
set -euo pipefail

export HF_HOME="$SCRATCH/hf_cache"
export HF_HUB_OFFLINE=1 TRANSFORMERS_OFFLINE=1   # pre-cache in a CPU job first
export OMP_NUM_THREADS=4 MKL_NUM_THREADS=4 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=4
export PYTORCH_CUDA_ALLOC_CONF=expandable_segments:True

SHARD="$SGE_TASK_ID"
OUT="$SCRATCH/vep/esm1v/shard_${SHARD}.parquet"

# skip-guard + lockfile: lets you broaden to other GPU models with a second
# array without duplicating work
[ -f "$OUT" ] && { echo "[skip] $OUT"; exit 0; }
LOCK="$OUT.lock"; mkdir "$LOCK" 2>/dev/null || { echo "[skip] claimed"; exit 0; }
trap 'rmdir "$LOCK" 2>/dev/null || true' EXIT

"$HOME/venvs/vep/bin/python" scripts/esm1v_masked_marginals.py \
    --positions "$SCRATCH/vep/positions.parquet" \
    --shard "$SHARD" --n-shards 20 \
    --models facebook/esm1v_t33_650M_UR90S_{1,2,3,4,5} \
    --window 1022 --batch-size 16 --out "$OUT"

Output schema per row: uniprot, pos, wt_aa, model_idx, the 20 log-probs, and cropped / window_start. Ensemble at analysis time as the mean over model_idx of logp[mut] − logp[wt] — keeping per-model scores lets you report ensemble variance, which is itself a useful uncertainty signal for the disagreement analysis.

Setup order — first week, in sequence
  1. Reclaim laptop disk. Nothing works until this is done.
  2. ssh hoffman2, run bash hoffman2/check_access.sh from a copy of the condenseq scaffold. Record which GPU models are reachable and whether highp applies to each team member.
  3. Create ~/venvs/vep: torch, transformers, protobuf (the tokenizer needs it and the failure message does not say so), duckdb, pyarrow, biotite, biopython.
  4. Submit a CPU job to pre-cache all five ESM-1v checkpoints into $SCRATCH/hf_cache. Not on the login node — it OOMs on the downloader.
  5. Verify an offline load on a compute node via qrsh -l gpu,A100,cuda=1,h_rt=2:00:00,h_data=8G -pe shared 4 before submitting any array.
  6. Run the Phase-0 spike on five proteins. Measure real GPU seconds per position and extrapolate before committing to the full run.

Note for the analysis stage: per your global working preferences, pin OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 MKL_NUM_THREADS=1 for the small-matrix sklearn work on the laptop. Inside a Hoffman2 job the opposite applies — match threads to $NSLOTS, which env.sh already handles.
